Halden Almanac of Natural Science (excerpt, fictional)

[BIO-1] Glimmerfen moss glows blue-green, but only in complete darkness. Its light comes from a pigment called lumeran, not from bacteria.
[BIO-2] Tidewater crabs molt twice a year, once in March and once in September. A freshly molted crab stays hidden for nine days.
[BIO-3] Saltreed grass survives in water up to three times saltier than the open sea. It stores the excess salt in its leaf tips and sheds them each autumn.
[GEO-1] The Karrow Ridge stands 4,200 meters tall. It was raised by volcanic uplift, not by two plates colliding.
[GEO-2] Lake Orrin is the deepest lake on the Halden continent at 1,100 meters. It never freezes, even at minus 30 degrees Celsius, because hot springs feed it from below.
[PHYS-1] Vantium is a metal that melts at 640 degrees Celsius and is lighter than aluminum. It conducts heat better than copper.
[PHYS-2] Sound travels through Mirelle gel at 2,100 meters per second, about six times its speed in air.
[ASTRO-1] The moon Tessaly orbits the gas giant Brume once every 41 hours. Tessaly is the largest of Brume's seven moons.
[ASTRO-2] The comet Wend returns every 76 years. Its tail always points away from the star Aurel, and its last pass was in the year 1987 of the Halden calendar.
[CHEM-1] Ferrol salt releases heat when it dissolves in water and turns the solution violet. The color fades after about an hour.
[CHEM-2] Quillon acid is stored only in amber-coated containers, because light breaks it down into harmless water and nitrogen.
[METEO-1] Hail in the Vessa valley forms only in summer. Winter precipitation there is always snow or sleet.
