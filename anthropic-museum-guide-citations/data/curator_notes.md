# Saltmarsh Maritime Museum: Curator's Notes, Gallery 3 ("The Wreck of the Marigold")

_Compiled by Dr. Ines Okonkwo-Hale, Senior Curator. Internal gallery notes, revised for the 2026 season._

## 3.1 The ship

The Marigold was a three-masted trading barque, launched at Harlow Quay in 1861
and lost off Gannet Rock on the night of 14 November 1887. She carried
Ceylon tea and 40 crates of Staffordshire crockery on her final voyage, bound
for Liverpool. Of the 23 crew aboard, 19 survived; the master, Captain Elias
Threlkeld, was among the four who did not.

## 3.2 The bell

The ship's bell (case 3A) is the only object in the gallery recovered on the
night of the wreck itself. A fisherman, Joss Pellow, hauled it from the shallows
at dawn with a boat hook. The bell is cracked from the lip to the crown. Please
do not describe the crack as "damage from the rocks": the 1888 inquiry blamed
the crack on a casting flaw, and the bell was already cracked before she sailed.
It is never rung. Visitors may not touch it, and the case alarm is very
sensitive.

## 3.3 The figurehead

The figurehead is a carved woman holding a sprig of marigolds. It was carved
by the Harlow Quay yard, probably by Tobias Wren, but there is no signed
record, so say "attributed to" and never "carved by". It is made of yellow pine
and has been repainted twice. The current colours were restored in 2019 from
paint scrapings. There is no truth in the story that the figurehead is cursed;
that tale was invented by a 1950s ghost-walk company, and we do not repeat it
as fact.

## 3.4 The crockery

Roughly 600 pieces of the Staffordshire cargo were raised during the 1974
survey dive. Only 31 are on display, in the long case by the window. The blue
willow-pattern plates are the most common; the single gilt-edged teapot is the
rarest piece and the reason the case is lit from below. Pieces from the cargo
are not for sale and are not loaned to other museums before 2030.

## 3.5 The sea chest

Seaman Davy Lowther's sea chest (case 3C) contains a pocket Bible, a clay pipe,
and a letter to his sister Hannah that he never posted. The letter is
displayed as a facsimile; the original is in cold storage because the ink is
fading. Lowther was 17 and one of the four who drowned.

## 3.6 Visiting Gallery 3

Gallery 3 is on the first floor and reached by the lift or the east stairs.
The gallery is step-free. The audio guide for this gallery runs 11 minutes.
Flash photography is not permitted anywhere in Gallery 3; photography without
flash is welcome. The gallery closes 30 minutes before the rest of the museum,
at 4:30 PM, so the dive footage can be rewound and the projector cooled.

## 3.7 Tone for guides

Guides should be warm but accurate. Do not speculate about how individual crew
members felt. Do not give dates or figures beyond those in these notes; if a
visitor asks something these notes do not cover, say you do not have that
information and suggest the front desk or the museum's research library.
